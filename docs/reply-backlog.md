**Now the one part that needs explaining, because a number in it looks worse
than it is.**

Search reaches inside the audio for any episode that has a transcript. When the
archive held 200 episodes, 147 had one. It now holds 785 and 174 do — **the
count went up, the proportion went down a lot.**

That's not something that went wrong. Transcripts only became routine in your
production a couple of years ago:

- 2026 — 59 of 68 episodes
- 2025 — 80 of 107
- 2024 — 18 of 107
- 2023 — 13 of 126
- 2022 — 4 of 118
- **2021 and earlier — none at all**

I checked all 785 against Podbean directly rather than assume it. **We hold
every transcript that exists**, bar one, where Podbean's own link is broken.

**So the older episodes are fully searchable** by title, description, guests,
tags and date. You just can't search *inside the audio* for them, because nobody
ever wrote down what was said.

**If that matters, we can have them transcribed.** It's the one job that needs
the recordings rather than the text, and it divides fairly naturally:

- **2022–2024** — about 316 episodes. Recent enough that you might replay them,
  and they sit alongside episodes that already have transcripts.
- **Before 2022** — 259 episodes. The oldest material, least likely to come
  round again.

No rush on this at all, and doing only the recent half is a perfectly good
answer. I'd rather price whichever you actually want than quote for all of it
out of habit — so have a think about how far back it's genuinely worth being
able to search the spoken audio.
